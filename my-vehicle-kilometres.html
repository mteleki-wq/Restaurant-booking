Guidance checked 25 Oct 2026

1. Check the journey

Normal travel from home to a regular college or workplace is generally private. An ABN, a long distance, a toll road or doing preparation at home does not, by itself, make commuting deductible.

Travel directly between workplaces, travel while performing duties and some travel to alternative workplaces may qualify. Specific exceptions for home-based work, itinerant work or essential bulky equipment need evidence and review.

The journal defaults to Private / commuting. If you mark a leg as eligible business travel, enter the specific basis. Exclude personal detours and expenses that have been reimbursed.

2. Use the vehicle's rules

Motorcycle: the car cents-per-kilometre method and statutory car logbook method do not apply. Claiming uses the work-related part of actual costs with receipts. A diary helps explain the split between private and eligible business use.

Car: eligible individuals and sole traders may use cents per kilometre (up to 5,000 eligible km per car per income year) or the logbook method. Companies and trusts use actual costs. This tool calculates kilometres, not dollar deductions.

Keep the motorcycle and car records separate. A rainy car day replaces a bike day; it is not an additional return trip.

3. Keep the evidence behind the total

  • Record dates, start and end places, specific purpose and kilometres for each real journey.
  • For a car logbook, record actual start/end odometer readings and start/end dates for each journey. Make entries at the end of the trip or as soon as possible afterwards.
  • Keep fuel and other expense receipts, service/registration records, toll statements and relevant teaching rosters or invoices.
  • A first car logbook normally needs at least 12 continuous weeks representative of use. A nine-week teaching term alone is too short. Consider the three-week break when choosing a representative period.
  • Keep logbook and relevant yearly odometer records, and review changes in your driving pattern. A qualifying car logbook may be used for up to five years while circumstances remain representative.
  • Records generally need to be retained for five years; some records, including logbooks relied on in later claims, need to be kept longer. Follow the ATO's retention rules for your claim.

4. Use estimates honestly

A repeated weekly pattern can explain a calculation, but it does not prove that every journey occurred or was deductible. Do not create invented odometer readings or describe a retrospective estimate as a contemporaneous logbook.

Drafts are excluded from recorded totals. A reconstructed entry is labelled separately and needs a supporting reference. Creation and edit timestamps remain in the JSON backup. An actual-record label is your statement, not independent verification.

For a car cents-per-kilometre claim, you still need to show how eligible kilometres were worked out. A reconstructed calculation may help explain that basis; your tax agent should assess the available evidence and method.
