A teaching proposal · AI in marketing and business

Teach marketing with the AI tools businesses are using now.

A short, practical module built around three real platforms designed and built here in Sydney. Students watch them working, test them, and critique them, so the marketing theory they’re learning becomes visible, and they learn to use AI with good judgement.

🎓Purely educational. The platforms are used as case studies only. Nothing is sold to students or the college, students need no accounts, and no student data is collected.
Why now

AI is already part of everyday marketing.

Small businesses now use AI to write emails, answer customers and run their websites. Our students will be asked to do the same, and to know when the AI is wrong.

01

Theory becomes visible

The customer journey, the marketing mix, segmentation and retention, shown on real screens instead of a textbook diagram.

02

Skills employers ask for

Briefing an AI tool, editing what it writes, setting up a chatbot’s knowledge, and planning a campaign people actually open.

03

Judgement, not just tools

Checking facts, protecting privacy, getting consent and keeping a human in charge. The habits that make AI safe to use at work.

Three live case studies

Three businesses. Three marketing lessons.

Each case study comes with short videos students can watch in class, plus a live example they can try on their own phones.

Case 1 · Hospitality

A restaurant and its guest journey

One independent restaurant: website, online booking, ordering at the table, then a follow-up email that brings the guest back.

  • The customer journey, end to end. From finding the restaurant online to the email that invites them back.
  • Consent-based email marketing. Only guests who said “keep me posted”, with an unsubscribe link in every email.
  • Segmentation and retention. A “we miss you” email for guests who haven’t returned, versus a new-menu email for everyone.
  • Consistent product and price. Change a dish once and the website, table menu and emails all match.
Class task: map the restaurant’s customer journey, then brief an AI tool to write the next campaign email and judge it against the brief.

Case 2 · Services

AI customer service and conversational marketing

A day spa, a clinic, a salon: clients book online and an AI assistant answers questions on the website, day and night.

  • What a chatbot should, and shouldn’t, say. It only uses facts the business gives it, and it says when it isn’t sure.
  • From question to lead. How a conversation turns into a booking, or a message the owner follows up.
  • Retention marketing. Rebooking reminders, offers and helpful tips. Value first, selling second.
  • Testing AI like a customer. Students ask it hard questions and record where it helps and where it fails.
Class task: chat with the demo spa’s assistant, write a test report, then write the “knowledge brief” an AI assistant would need for a local business.

Case 3 · Platforms and personal brand

Marketing to two audiences at once

ChefLink has to win chefs and venues at the same time. That makes it a clear way to teach value propositions, personal branding and relationship marketing.

  • Two value propositions. What a chef wants versus what a venue wants, and how one website speaks to both.
  • Personal branding. A chef’s portfolio of dishes, story and skills, built for a hiring audience.
  • CRM and outreach. Templates, tone and follow-up, and when to personalise rather than automate.
Class task: write a value proposition for each audience, then use AI to draft two outreach messages and compare their tone.

Session plan

Four sessions, or one workshop.

Each session pairs a short video with hands-on work, and each one produces evidence that can support assessment.

1

AI in the marketing mix

Watch the restaurant and service case studies. In groups, map each platform to the customer journey and the marketing mix, and spot where AI is doing the work.

Evidence: annotated customer-journey map
2

Write it with AI, then edit it

Brief an AI tool to draft a campaign email for a real scenario (a new menu, an event, a rebooking reminder). Edit it for brand voice, accuracy and consent rules.

Evidence: brief, AI draft and final copy, with a short rationale
3

Conversational marketing

Test a live AI assistant with real and tricky questions. Then write the knowledge brief and the “say this, never say that” rules for a business of their choice.

Evidence: chatbot test report and knowledge brief
4

Plan, measure, improve

Build a small communication plan: audience, channel, message, timing and the measure of success (opens, bookings, replies), with a responsible-AI checklist.

Evidence: one-page marketing communication plan
Running through every session

Using AI responsibly.

Students learn the habits that make AI safe to use in a business, not just the buttons.

Consent firstEmail only people who agreed, with a working unsubscribe link (Spam Act 2003).
Protect privacyWhat customer data should never go into an AI tool, and why.
Check the factsAI can be confidently wrong. Every price, date and claim gets checked.
Be honest about AITell customers when they’re talking to an assistant, and give them a person to contact.
Inclusive languageSpot bias and stereotypes in AI drafts and fix them.
A human signs offThe business owns the message. AI drafts, people decide.
